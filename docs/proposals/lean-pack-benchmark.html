Lean Pack Benchmark

E5 grew into a four-night campaign. This design answers the same question in about 2 hours of run and 2 hours of grading, using everything that is already built.

Revision 2 targets about 2 h of run and 2 h of grading (operator, 2026-10-09). Revision 1 proposed 3 repetitions (180 cells, about 3.4 h + 3.5 h).

The question

With the AI-Forward pack on versus off, does each harness (Claude Code, Codex, Copilot) do better on enterprise-quality tasks, and what does it cost in tokens?

Why E5 got so big

The plan asks for a separate verdict for every property × harness pair: 5 properties × 3 harnesses = 15 verdicts. Each verdict must detect a 20-point pass-rate difference (MDE 0.20). The spec's power formula says that needs 53 paired runs per verdict. With 2 tasks per property, that is 27 repetitions of every cell.

The multi-night grid, the alarm and drill, the nightly resume, the two-stage power analysis and the convergence proofs all follow from that one choice. The question above does not need 15 separate verdicts at that precision.

The figure 53 was checked here by recomputing it with the spec's paired-binary formula (ψ = 0.28, α = 0.05, power 0.8), which gives 52.5. The other detectable effects on this page come from the same formula.

Proposed: one paired run, about 2 h + 2 h

The run

  • The same 10 tasks (2 per property), 2 arms and 3 harnesses, with the models pinned as today.
  • 2 repetitions: 10 × 2 × 3 × 2 = 120 cells.
  • Two batches of 60, one repetition each. Batch 1 is the existing pilot ring, and it measures the real time and tokens before batch 2 starts.
  • About 2.2 h of run at 3 slots, then about 2.3 h of grading. Inferred
  • Under 4 hours of run, so no alarm, no drill and no overnight.

The answer it gives

  • Per harness: pack-on vs pack-off pass rate over the 10 tasks. That is 20 pairs each, enough to detect a 31-point difference: a large effect.
  • All harnesses together: 60 pairs, enough to detect a 19-point difference.
  • Per property: shown as a direction with its counts (4 pairs per harness), labelled exploratory. These are not verdicts.
  • Cost: the on/off token ratio per harness, with its interval. Token ratios are large (the spec's figures: pack-on used 2.2× the tokens on Claude Code, 3.1× on Codex and 10.8× on Copilot), so 20 pairs measure them well even with the 24% run-to-run drift the spec records.

If one harness lands near the line and you care about it, top it up with 1 more repetition for that harness only: 20 cells, about 22 min of run and 23 min of grading. That takes it to 30 pairs, enough to detect a 26-point difference. You decide on the numbers in front of you, not in advance.

What was reduced to reach 2 h, and why it is safe

About 2 h of run at 3 slots is about 107 cells, and about 2 h of grading is about 103 cells (grid-4 rates). Each lever was weighed against the objectives: every harness compared, every property covered, the arms paired, and tokens measured.

The detectable effects assume every pair is independent. Repeats of one task are not fully independent, so the real per-harness figure sits somewhere between the 20-pair value (31 points) and the 10-task value (42 points). That is why the design keeps all 10 tasks and cuts repetitions instead.

What changes

drop for this benchmark

  • The multi-night grid, the alarm task and the drill. The HB-CMP-011 rule never triggers at under 4 hours of run.
  • 15 separate property × harness verdicts at MDE 0.20.
  • The two power analyses and the admission loop between them.
  • Operator stops A and B as two steps. You approve the token spend once, before batch 1.
  • The forced "defect fix with re-grade" exercise.
  • The convergence re-run (resume table + TLC). It proves multi-night resume, which this run does not use.
  • The 24 calibration cells.

keep

  • The frozen engine identity and the final task records, so results are reproducible.
  • The pinned models and the paired design (same task, same repetition, arm is the only difference).
  • Ruling 116: pack-off workspaces carry no instruction files, checked by HB-PRE-009 for every harness.
  • Grading and the report. Tokens are the only cost axis (Ruling 115).
  • A short written pre-registration: one paragraph committed before batch 1, naming the primary metric (pass rate) and the comparisons. That keeps the result honest at almost no cost.

Nothing already built is deleted. The campaign machinery stays in the code, unused by this run, and is available if a later question needs per-property precision.
